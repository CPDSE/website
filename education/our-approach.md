  - color: soft-white
    title: What is Data Science
    intro: Data science is not just a set of tools — it is a way of thinking about evidence, uncertainty, and decisions.
    content: |
      At CPDSE we define data science as the discipline of extracting reliable knowledge from data using principled methods. In a pharmaceutical context this means working systematically with clinical and preclinical data, real-world evidence, and administrative health records to answer scientific and regulatory questions.

      Three capabilities define the data-literate pharmaceutical professional:

      - **Quantitative reasoning** — understanding variation, uncertainty, and statistical inference
      - **Computational fluency** — writing and reading code well enough to automate, reproduce, and share analyses
      - **Domain integration** — translating data findings into meaningful conclusions for drug development, regulation, or patient care

      Our work is grounded in the belief that these capabilities should be taught as an integrated whole — not as separate add-ons to an already full curriculum.

  - color: ivory
    title: Examples
    intro: Data science in pharmaceutical education takes many forms. Here are concrete examples of what it looks like across different parts of the field.
    cards:
      - title: Pharmacoepidemiology
        content: Using Danish register data to study drug utilisation patterns and adverse effects at population scale — with R or Python replacing manual spreadsheet workflows.
      - title: Pharmacometrics
        content: Building and evaluating PK/PD models with nonlinear mixed-effects methods. Students learn to interpret model diagnostics and understand the assumptions behind common software outputs.
      - title: Drug Regulatory Science
        content: Applying reproducible analysis pipelines to support benefit–risk assessment. Emphasis on transparent reporting, version control, and structured data management.
      - title: Cheminformatics
        content: Predicting molecular properties from chemical structure using machine-learning models. Students work with real compound databases and learn the trade-offs between interpretability and predictive power.
      - title: Real-World Evidence
        content: Designing and executing observational studies from electronic health records. Covers causal inference concepts such as confounding, effect modification, and target trial emulation.
      - title: Data Visualisation
        content: Communicating complex statistical results to scientific and lay audiences. Students produce publication-quality graphics using ggplot2 or matplotlib and learn the principles behind effective data storytelling.

  - color: soft-white
    title: Our Competency Model
    intro: CPDSE uses a structured competency model to design and evaluate educational offerings at all levels — from bachelor courses to PhD programmes and continuing education.
    content: |
      The model organises pharmaceutical data science competencies into three tiers:

      **Foundation**
      Core literacy that every pharmaceutical graduate should possess — reading and interpreting statistical analyses, understanding data provenance, and recognising common pitfalls in quantitative reasoning.

      **Practitioner**
      Applied skills for working professionals and advanced students — writing reproducible analysis code, handling messy real-world datasets, choosing appropriate statistical methods, and communicating results clearly.

      **Expert**
      Research-level expertise for those who develop new methods or lead data-intensive projects — advanced modelling, causal inference, machine-learning applications, and the ability to critically evaluate and extend the field.

      Each educational offering maps explicitly onto one or more tiers, so learners and educators can track progress and identify gaps. The model is reviewed regularly as the field evolves.
