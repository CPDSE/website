# ── Section 1: Why data science for pharmacists ──────────────────
section1_eyebrow: "Why data science for pharmacists"
section1_title: "You don't need to become a programmer. You need to speak both languages."
section1_intro: "Pharmaceutical science generates more data than ever — from high-throughput screening to real-world patient records. The students and researchers who can work with that data directly, without waiting for a specialist, are at a fundamental advantage. CPDSE exists to close that gap."

why_cards:
  - title: Lab data analysis
    content: Summarise and visualise experimental results from assays, dissolution tests, or stability studies — without exporting to Excel first.
  - title: Image processing
    content: Analyse microscopy, particle, and formulation images with reproducible, automated workflows.
  - title: Simulation
    content: Model drug release profiles, pharmacokinetics, or molecular interactions computationally — before running a single experiment.
  - title: Clinical & real-world data
    content: Work with patient records, trial datasets, and population-level health data using the same tools used in research.
  - title: Reproducible reporting
    content: Combine analysis, results, and interpretation in a single shareable notebook — so your work can be checked, reused, and built on.
  - title: Free & open tools
    content: Python, R, Julia — industry-standard, actively maintained, and free. No licence fees, no vendor lock-in.

# ── Section 2: Pull quote ─────────────────────────────────────────
blockquote: "The goal is not to replace the statistician or the programmer — it's to stop being entirely dependent on them."

# ── Section 3: What data science actually is ──────────────────────
section3_eyebrow: "What data science actually is"
section3_title: "Not one skill. A combination of seven."
section3_intro: "Data science is often treated as synonymous with machine learning or coding. In reality it is a broad competency spanning ethics, mathematics, computing, data management, analysis, AI, and communication. Pharmaceutical scientists need literacy across all seven — not mastery of any single one."

competencies:
  - "Ethics · Legislation · Privacy"
  - "Mathematics & Statistics"
  - "Computing & Programming"
  - "Data Acquisition & Management"
  - "Exploration · Mining · Analysis"
  - "Machine Learning & AI"
  - "Visualization & Presentation"

# ── Section 4: How to teach it ───────────────────────────────────
section4_eyebrow: "How to teach it"
section4_title: "Learning by doing, not by watching."
section4_intro: "Lectures about data science don't produce data scientists. CPDSE teaching formats are built around practice — small tasks, real datasets, and immediate feedback. Students learn by running code, not reading about it."

teach_cards:
  - title: Coding exercises
    bullets:
      - Python, R, or both
      - Small, focused tasks
      - Each building on the last
  - title: Lab reports in notebooks
    bullets:
      - Replace Word with Jupyter or Quarto
      - Integrate code, results, and interpretation
      - Directly exportable and reproducible
  - title: Interactive apps
    bullets:
      - Build Shiny or Streamlit apps
      - Turn an analysis into a shareable tool
      - No front-end experience needed
  - title: Real dataset challenges
    bullets:
      - Use actual pharmaceutical datasets
      - Explore, clean, and model real data
      - Present findings to the group
  - title: Reproduce published results
    bullets:
      - Take a published paper
      - Recreate the analysis from raw data
      - Understand methods by doing, not reading
  - title: Collaborative data projects
    bullets:
      - Work in teams on a shared analysis
      - Use Git for version control
      - Learn professional research workflows
