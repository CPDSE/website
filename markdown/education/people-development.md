      Universities are built to develop formal education. AI is not waiting for it. By the time a new tool has made it into a curriculum, the tool has changed twice.

      People Development closes that gap for the people already in post — researchers, educators, technical and administrative staff. No ECTS, no exams, no lectures about the future of AI. You bring a real task from your own work and you leave with something that runs. This complements existing university courses and training offerings; it does not replace them.

      There are two ways in. Pick the one that matches the time you actually have.

  - color: soft-white
    id: format
    eyebrow: The format
    title: "What one hour looks like"
    intro: "Every session follows the same four-step rhythm, so you always know what you are walking into. It is the 4C model from Sharon Bowman's <em>Training from the BACK of the Room</em> — the room does the work, not the person standing at the front."
    card_columns: 2
    card_color: soft-white
    cards:
      - num: "01"
        title: Connections
        content: |
          Ten minutes. We start from what the room already knows. You put a real task from your own week on the table and find out who else has the same one.
      - num: "02"
        title: Concepts
        content: |
          Fifteen minutes. New material in short chunks — never more than a few minutes before you do something with it. Just enough to use the tool well, and no more.
      - num: "03"
        title: Concrete Practice
        content: |
          Twenty-five minutes. You practise the skill on your own material, on your own machine, and then teach what you found to the person next to you.
      - num: "04"
        title: Conclusions
        content: |
          Ten minutes. You summarise what you learned, decide what changes next week, and say it out loud before you leave.
