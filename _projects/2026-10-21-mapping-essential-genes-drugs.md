## Background

We still don't know what thousands of human genes do. One powerful clue comes from large public experiments in which every gene was switched off, one at a time, in hundreds of cancer cell lines. [Genes that "behave alike" when lost usually work together](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-021-00840-z), so their patterns reveal hidden partnerships.
 
Our lab turned this idea into [HIDDENdb](https://bofillderoslab.shinyapps.io/hiddendb/), a free, interactive web tool that maps these gene partnerships ([preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06903)). It already rediscovers known protein complexes and points to roles for genes nobody has studied.
 
Now we want to add **drugs** to the map. The same cell lines have been tested against thousands of compounds. If a drug kills cells in the same pattern as losing a particular gene, that gene is a strong candidate for how the drug works. In this project you will connect drugs to genes, uncover how medicines act.

## What you'll do

- Combine a public drug-response dataset (PRISM) with the gene data in HIDDENdb
- Find drugs whose effects mirror the loss of specific genes, and check the method on drugs with known targets
- Pick a few new drug–gene links worth testing in the lab
- Add a "Drugs" tab to the [HIDDENdb](https://github.com/Bofill-De-Ros-Lab/HIDDENdb) web app
